\chapter{Linear attention and long convolutions}
\label{ch:linattn}

\newthought{Two more lines of attack} on the \(n^2\) cost don't go through
either fixed-pattern sparsity or state-space recurrence. They take the
attention equation and reformulate it.

\section{Linear attention via kernel feature maps}

Standard attention computes
\begin{equation}
  o_t \;=\; \sum_{s \le t} \frac{\exp(q_t \cdot k_s / \sqrt{\dhead})}
                                {\sum_{s' \le t} \exp(q_t \cdot k_{s'} / \sqrt{\dhead})}\, v_s.
\end{equation}
The \(\exp\) of a dot product is the kernel that makes everything
quadratic — each \(q_t\) has to be paired with every \(k_s\). What if we
replace it with a kernel \(\phi(q) \cdot \phi(k)\) for some explicit
feature map \(\phi : \R^{\dhead} \to \R^{m}\)?

Then, dropping the normaliser briefly,
\begin{equation}
  o_t \;=\; \sum_{s \le t} \phi(q_t) \cdot \phi(k_s)\, v_s
        \;=\; \phi(q_t)\T \underbrace{\sum_{s \le t} \phi(k_s)\, v_s\T}_{S_t}.
\end{equation}
The sum \(S_t \in \R^{m \times \dhead}\) can be maintained
incrementally: \(S_t = S_{t-1} + \phi(k_t)\, v_t\T\). One outer product
per token, one matvec per token. Total cost: \(\bigO(n \cdot m \cdot
\dhead)\). Linear in \(n\). \citet{katharopoulos2020linear} dubbed this
\emph{linear attention} and showed the equivalence to a fast linear-RNN.

\paragraph{What goes wrong.} The choice of \(\phi\) is the whole game. The
exact \(\exp\) kernel cannot be written as a finite \(\phi(q) \cdot \phi(k)\)
in any low-dimensional space.\sidenote{The Performer
\citep{choromanski2021performer} uses random feature maps that approximate
the softmax kernel, with provable accuracy bounds. Other proposals use
ReLU, ELU, or learned positive feature maps. None matches the empirical
quality of full softmax attention at scale.} In practice, linear attention
underperforms exact attention by a meaningful amount, especially on
associative-recall tasks.

\paragraph{Modern revival: gated linear attention.} RetNet
\citep{sun2023retnet}, GLA, and the Mamba-2 family all use
linear-attention machinery with gating to recover some of the lost
expressivity. They have become a standard ingredient in hybrids
(Chapter~\ref{ch:hybrids}).

\section{Long convolutions and Hyena}

The other reformulation is to drop attention's all-pairs structure and
replace it with a \emph{very long, learned convolutional kernel}.
Convolutions are linear in \(n\) when computed with the FFT
(\(\bigO(n \log n)\) per layer for kernel length \(n\)).

Hyena \citep{poli2023hyena} composes implicit (parametrised by an MLP),
data-dependent (gated by the input), long convolutions to build a sequence
mixer that scales sub-quadratically. The convolutional kernels are
\emph{long enough} to span the entire context, addressing the receptive-field
limitation of standard CNN-based language models from the 2010s.

\paragraph{Trade-offs.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Asymptote.} \(\bigO(n \log n)\) — better than transformers,
worse than pure linear-attention, but the FFT constants are friendly.
\item \textbf{Routing is again content-light.} The kernel is a function of
position (and, in Hyena, a gate function of the input), but it does not
\emph{select} which positions to attend to in the way a softmax does.
\item \textbf{Quality.} Hyena and friends are competitive on long-range
benchmarks at small scale; the picture at frontier scale is still being
written.
\end{itemize}

\keyidea{Linear attention and long convolutions both win the
\(n\)-dependence by giving up the precise dot-product routing of
softmax attention. They tend to live in hybrids rather than as pure
replacements.}
